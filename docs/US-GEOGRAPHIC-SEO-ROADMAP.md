# U.S. Geographic SEO Roadmap: States, Metro Areas and Counties

**Status:** working strategy, 9 October 2026  
**Goal:** build relevant organic visibility for Accounstone's U.S. accounting, bookkeeping and tax-preparation support across selected states and local markets. This is a roadmap, not a claim that any page ranks or that demand has been measured.

## The governing rule

We want to rank in relevant U.S. states, metro areas and counties, but we should not publish a page for every state, county or city just because the URL can be created. Google identifies substantially similar regional pages made mainly to capture queries and funnel visitors as potential doorway abuse. Every local page must be genuinely useful on its own.

Reference: Google Search Central, Spam Policies — Doorway abuse: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies

## Geographic page hierarchy

### Tier 1 — National service and industry pages

These are the main commercial foundations:
- `/markets/united-states` — U.S. market and delivery model.
- U.S. service pages — bookkeeping, accounting, tax preparation, AP, AR, payroll and audit support.
- Industry pages — CPA firms, HOA accounting, property management, real estate and other industries where the firm has a real service offer.

Each page should own a distinct intent and link to the most relevant supporting pages.

### Tier 2 — State pages

Current state pages:
- `/markets/united-states/texas`
- `/markets/united-states/california`
- `/markets/united-states/florida`

The national U.S. page links to these pages. Before adding another state, assess:
1. Is this a market Accounstone is ready and able to serve?
2. Is there a distinct accounting workflow or customer segment to address?
3. Can we provide accurate, current state context without drifting into unsupported tax/legal advice?
4. Can the page offer genuinely useful information beyond changing the state name?
5. Can the relevant industry/service pages link to it naturally?

State pages must describe remote service coverage accurately and must not imply a local office.

### Tier 3 — Metro, city and county pages

Create these selectively, after a state or industry cluster is useful and well connected. Examples of *page concepts to validate*, not pages already approved or evidence of search demand:
- HOA accounting for community associations in a target county/metro.
- Property-management accounting for a target metro's residential/commercial portfolio operators.
- Real-estate accounting for owners, investors or developers in a market.
- A software + workflow + location page when the combination has a real use case.

A county page should answer local questions that a statewide page cannot. Depending on the target, that could mean the types of associations or portfolios served, relevant public/local context with reliable sources, practical reporting workflows, the exact services available, and a clear route to the matching industry page. Do not imply local staff, a local address, local clients or local credentials unless true and documented.

If the only unique element is the county name, **do not publish the page**.

## Recommended rollout

### Phase 1 — Strengthen existing pages

1. Review the Texas, California and Florida pages for accuracy, usefulness and duplication.
2. Improve links from the national U.S. page and relevant industry/service pages.
3. Keep HOA, property-management and real-estate accounting distinct.
4. Review the existing Yardi + Texas page to ensure the software workflow and Texas context add unique value.
5. Check canonical URLs, sitemap inclusion, metadata and structured data for every page.

### Phase 2 — Select a limited pilot geography

Choose one state/metro/county cluster at a time based on business priorities and evidence, not a generic list of all U.S. counties. Score candidates using:
- Confirmed serviceability and operational fit.
- Existing clients, enquiries or relationships in the area, where the business can substantiate these.
- Evidence of relevant search demand from keyword research or Search Console when available.
- A real differentiator in the local page.
- Ability to build natural links from relevant industry, service and educational pages.
- Competitive difficulty and the strength of currently surfaced competitor pages.

Do not invent search volume, ranking difficulty or local market share. Record the source and date for each demand signal.

### Phase 3 — Publish and measure the pilot

For each page, record:
- Target query family and one primary intent.
- What makes the page materially different from the state and national pages.
- Sources for local facts and the date checked.
- Internal links in and out.
- Canonical URL, indexability and sitemap status.
- Leads, relevant impressions/clicks and conversions once reliable data becomes available.

Expand only when the pilot demonstrates useful coverage and a repeatable content standard. A published page is not proof of indexing or ranking.

## Local-page quality checklist

- [ ] The page represents a real service Accounstone can deliver remotely.
- [ ] The state/county/metro is the intended target, not a claim of local presence.
- [ ] The opening paragraph clearly says who the service is for and what work is supported.
- [ ] Local details are accurate, useful and sourced where factual.
- [ ] The page contains meaningful differences from nearby location pages.
- [ ] The title and H1 reflect one primary intent naturally.
- [ ] The page links to the right national service and industry page.
- [ ] No unsupported claims about local offices, clients, credentials, prices or outcomes.
- [ ] It is canonical, indexable and included in the sitemap if it deserves indexing.
- [ ] Its value can be explained without saying only “we serve [place].”

## Competitor research standard

Use competitor pages to identify:
- Which service tasks they explain.
- Which customer types and software they address.
- What questions, proof and engagement details buyers may need.
- How they organize national, state and industry coverage.

Do **not** treat a page found in a web search as proof it ranks first. Search results vary by location and time. Do not copy competitor claims, ratings, prices, credentials or testimonials. The companion `docs/COMPETITOR-SEO-BENCHMARK.md` records the qualitative observations and source links.

## Decision for the current site

The immediate priority is **not** to create hundreds of county pages. First improve the existing national, industry and three state pages; then select a small number of state/metro/county opportunities that match actual serviceability and have evidence of demand. This builds a scalable U.S. geographic cluster without producing thin doorway pages.
