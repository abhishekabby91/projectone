# Competitor SEO Review — October 2026

## Purpose and limits

This is a qualitative review of competitor pages surfaced in web search on 9 October 2026. It is **not** a verified ranking report: search results vary by location, device, query and personalization, and a search result alone does not establish a page's stable ranking or traffic. The review uses visible page structure and service coverage to identify useful content patterns. It does not copy competitor wording or treat competitors' marketing claims as verified facts.

## Patterns worth applying

### 1. Keep each page aligned to a distinct reader

- **Real estate accounting:** property owners, investors, developers and commercial operators; property- and entity-level books, reconciliations, lender/investor reporting and month-end close.
- **Property management accounting:** property managers; tenant and owner ledgers, rent-roll activity, deposits, CAM/recoverable costs, AP/AR and owner statements.
- **HOA accounting:** association boards and community managers; assessments, homeowner ledgers, operating and reserve funds, delinquency schedules and monthly board packs.
- **CPA firms:** preparation capacity, review-ready workpapers, cleanup and defined workflows inside the firm's systems.

Do not merge these pages into one generic real-estate outsourcing page or create location variants by changing only place names.

### 2. Name the records and recurring tasks

Competitor pages often explain the underlying work rather than relying only on a broad list of services. Accounstone pages should keep explaining what is maintained, reconciled and delivered: property/entity ledgers, assessment and homeowner balances, tenant/deposit records, AP/AR aging, bank reconciliations, CAM schedules, month-end close and reporting packs.

Use these only where they match the actual engagement scope. Do not imply that Accounstone collects rent, controls client funds, approves payments, gives legal advice, or makes reserve-funding decisions.

### 3. Mention software in context, without unsupported certification claims

Property-management buyers care whether an accounting workflow can fit the systems already in use. The property-management page now names AppFolio alongside Yardi, QuickBooks, Xero, Sage and NetSuite, while retaining the requirement to confirm the exact product, access and workflow during scoping. This is not a claim of software certification or implementation capability.

### 4. Make the process and responsibilities clear

Useful commercial pages explain how records move from transaction processing to reconciliation, close and reporting, and which approvals remain with the client. Keep these explanations concrete and specific to each audience rather than repeating generic outsourcing claims.

### 5. Make FAQs answer real buyer questions

FAQs should clarify scope, systems, catch-up work, property/entity separation, reporting and handoff. Keep answers consistent with the visible page and the service actually offered. Structured data must reflect the visible FAQ; adding schema alone does not guarantee a search enhancement.
